# Service Court interior visual review

**Scope:** independent visual review of the supplied current Tiled security-furnishing overview and one native in-game settled-roof office capture. This is a bounded review of two JPEG previews, not whole-level or product acceptance.

## Observed strengths and defects

- The enlarged security complex reads as multiple connected interior zones: a broad archive, a south office/lobby area and a long east corridor. It no longer reads as a shallow exterior kiosk.
- The room remains visually under-furnished for its size. The archive shelving repeats in evenly spaced paired rows across a large unbroken floor. The office/lobby have a few desks, chairs and lockers, but do not yet establish a strong security-control/workspace center. The east corridor is mostly bare.
- Small props appear as isolated marks on the floor, with no visible relationship to work surfaces or wall edges. The utility group at the far east edge looks detached from the interior's furnishings in this overview. More objects alone would not explain how the rooms were used.
- In the native capture, the settled roof and the player's local light pool are visible, but much of the room furniture and wall dressing recede into darkness. The HUD shows the security-room hold prompt; in this frame the corresponding physical control is not an obvious landmark at the shown scale.

## Concrete improvements

1. **Make the archive feel organized and traversable.** Keep shelves in deliberate perimeter runs and a few purposeful islands, but vary the row lengths and gaps. Preserve one broad route from the corridor into the archive and onward to the office/lobby. Use a restrained existing ground/material cue at the principal aisle and door thresholds instead of adding decorative floor marks.
2. **Create a readable security work center.** Cluster the control terminal, desk, chair, nearby lockers and radio/electrical box around the control side of the office. Give the player a clear approach and room to stand at the interaction. Keep surface details such as papers on desks and wall units attached to walls; keep them without movement hitboxes as authored.
3. **Give the corridor and entrances practical purpose.** Place a small number of utility items against wall edges or near a door where they explain the service route. Align each canopy, wall fixture and wall-side cluster with the matching authored doorway/footprint. Do not narrow the escape path or put clutter across the room's connecting doors.
4. **Check dark-play readability around the interaction.** Use the existing authored fixtures and lighting event path to make the control, its approach and the two retreat/continuation routes identifiable when the roof is settled. Review the player-facing and away-facing views; keep shadows and walls meaningful.

## Limits and evidence still needed

The Tiled overview does not show gameplay lighting, roof fade, actor scale, line-of-sight, collision or pursuit. The native capture is one static view and does not demonstrate the full room route, target interaction, doorway access, or crowd movement. Both files are lossy JPEG previews; they cannot establish exact pixel edges, transparency, pivots or collision footprints. Review a current matching render and executable at gameplay scale, then verify measured usable floor area and clearances against the authored wall/furniture footprints. Human navigation and pacing remain open.
